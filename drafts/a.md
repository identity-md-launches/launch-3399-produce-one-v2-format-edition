LEAD: South Korea drafts a crypto law that caps exchange ownership at 20%.

The Financial Services Commission unveiled the bill on 24 November 2026, according to Cointelegraph. It would hand won stablecoins to bank-majority groups and lift the 2017 ban on domestic token sales. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today]

Cointelegraph reports that the commission published the draft on 24 November 2026 and set out its terms in an announcement. The paper could not open that announcement, so every term below rests on Cointelegraph's account. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today]

The exchange rules carry the sharpest edge. Shareholders' stakes in a crypto exchange would be capped at 20%, or 34% with commission approval. Exchanges would also need separate licences for dealing, brokerage and custody, and would have to meet capital and operating requirements. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today]

Read plainly, a 20% ceiling would leave any larger holder two options: sell down or seek the 34% exception. Cointelegraph's account does not say how long holders would have to comply. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today]

Stablecoins would start inside the banking system. Banks would have to own more than half of an issuer collectively, though a fintech or technology company could be the largest single shareholder. Issuers would need commission authorisation and at least 5 billion won in equity, about $3.7 million at Cointelegraph's conversion. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today]

Reserves would have to cover at least 100% of tokens outstanding. Holders would be entitled to redemption at face value without delay. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today]

The draft also seeks to lift the ban on domestic initial coin offerings that has stood since 2017. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today] It is a draft. Cointelegraph's account names no date for a vote in the National Assembly, and the paper found none. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today]

[NEWS] CFTC staff let derivatives venues count bitcoin and ether toward operating reserves.
The Division of Market Oversight and the Division of Clearing and Risk issued the advisory on 23 November 2026. Bitcoin, ether and payment stablecoins may count toward the requirement to hold 12 months of operating costs, subject to haircuts. They may not count toward the separate liquidity requirement, because accounting rules do not treat them as cash or highly liquid securities. The advisory states staff views only and is not a no-action position. [https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9331-26]

[NEWS] Coinkite publishes a postmortem on the Coldcard recovery-phrase flaw.
Coinkite's 23 November 2026 postmortem says a software error made Coldcard wallets generate recovery phrases from a weaker source than designed, according to Cointelegraph. Coinkite said at least 15 independent reviews missed the flaw, because the phrases looked normal and passed some tests. Galaxy Research had reported in August 2026 that thefts linked to the flaw took at least 1,789 BTC, then worth about $114.7 million. Coinkite said it fixed the error on 31 July 2026. [https://cointelegraph.com/news/what-happened-in-crypto-today]

TECH:

[TECH] Leo Lara writes Ethereum's consensus rules as a diff in Lean 4.
On 23 November 2026, Leo Lara described EthCLLib, a Lean 4 package started in June 2026 that encodes the Fulu, Gloas and Heze forks. Each fork declares its parent in one line and writes only what changes, inheriting the rest. The earlier versions pass the reference test vectors of Ethereum's Python specification. The aim is a spec that client authors can read and that provers can reason about. [https://ethresear.ch/t/ethcllib-a-lean-4-framework-for-the-ethereum-consensus-specs/26283]

[TECH] Benedikt Wagner trades speed for a stronger proof in post-quantum sampling.
On 24 November 2026, Benedikt Wagner proposed a change to Ethereum's post-quantum data availability sampling design, which lets nodes check data without downloading it whole. He says the existing proof rules out structural weaknesses but yields a quantitatively weak security level. His variant carries a tighter bound, proven in Lean, at the cost of efficiency. [https://ethresear.ch/t/post-quantum-das-with-improved-provable-security/26285]
