LEAD: South Korea proposes reopening domestic token sales.

South Korea's Financial Services Commission presented a bill on 24 November 2026 that would let issuers sell tokens to the public at home. The proposal would also limit ownership of exchanges and start stablecoin issuance with bank-led groups. [https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010106/88199]

Issuers seeking buyers among the general public would first file a white paper with the commission. The filing would identify the seller and explain the project, the supply, the risks and how the issuer intends to spend the money. The regulator could demand corrections, and sellers would have to wait through a cooling-off period after publication before soliciting buyers. [https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010106/88199]

Exchanges would carry a disclosure duty of their own. They would file and publish a report when listing a token and disclose material changes, including hacks and changes in circulating supply, immediately. The bill would also make digital asset firms liable for customer losses from hacks or system failures without requiring customers to prove fault. [https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010106/88199]

The commission proposes a standard cap of 20% of an exchange operator's shares. It plans to allow holdings of up to 34% with its approval. Existing exchanges would get three years from designation before the cap applies, with a possible extension for smaller operators. [https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010106/88199]

The regulator plans to start stablecoin issuance with consortia in which banks collectively hold a majority. An issuer would have to set aside assets equal to at least the full value of tokens outstanding and redeem holders promptly. The commission said it would support the bill through legislative debate. [https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010106/88199]

[NEWS] CFTC staff say derivatives firms can count some crypto toward operating reserves.
A 23 November 2026 advisory covers swap venues, futures exchanges and derivatives clearinghouses that must set aside enough to cover a year of operating costs. Staff say bitcoin, ether and payment stablecoins can count after discounts for risk. Futures exchanges and clearinghouses still need at least six months of costs in unencumbered cash or highly liquid securities. [https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9331-26] [https://www.cftc.gov/csl/26-31/download]

TECH:

[TECH] A researcher strengthens the proof behind a proposed Ethereum data check.
Benedikt Wagner published a tighter mathematical bound on 24 November 2026 for a design that checks whether sampled pieces belong to a single valid encoding. The revised design requires the prover to generate two proofs where the original required one. Wagner checked the bound in Lean under classical assumptions and left the corresponding quantum bound open. [https://ethresear.ch/t/post-quantum-das-with-improved-provable-security/26285]

[TECH] Csaba Király tests how Ethereum nodes could share combined proofs.
Király's 18 November 2026 simulation compared ways for nodes to share proofs that cover several transactions at once. Four of five designs handled 72 private transactions per second when all 1,000 simulated nodes combined proofs. With a single builder doing the work, those designs handled six to nine per second. [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263]

The single builder used an uplink half as fast as the recommendation for local block builders. [https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8288-the-networking-side-of-recursive-aggregation/26263]
